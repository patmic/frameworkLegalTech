Dear Academic Editor,

Thank you for a report that was demanding in the most useful way. All five
major points identified real weaknesses, and addressing them improved the
paper: the delimitation requested in 3.3 forced us to state the direction of
validation precisely, which turned out to be the clearest expression of what
the contribution is.

Please note one change in the title, which we make explicit so that it is not
mistaken for an inconsistency between versions:

  Previous: "... for Semantic Validation of Judicial Digital Ecosystems"
  Revised:  "... for Semantic Validation of LegalTech Ecosystems"

The revised wording places the paper within the LegalTech research line to
which it belongs and aligns the title with the governance ontology the
instrument validates against, in which the LegalTech domain is one of the ten
dimensions. No claim in the paper changes as a consequence.

Summary of the revisions:

- 3.1 External validity. A dedicated block separates what generalises from what
  does not: the numerical result describes one institution over a one-month
  window of 17 runs; the diagnostic pattern is stated as a hypothesis only a
  multi-institutional study can test; only the instrument generalises. A
  four-step transfer protocol names the one step carrying real cost.

- 3.2 Scoring and mapping. A new subsection states what Gamma is: a versioned,
  deterministic table of signal-to-class rules, each firing on a syntactic
  condition re-evaluable on the stored snapshot, with concrete instances. We
  also clarified that the ontology drives the acquisition, so Gamma is that
  same rule table read in the opposite direction. onto(d) and Psi(d) are now
  distinguished by epistemic status, which enables partial replication by a
  reviewer who rejects our rubric.

- 3.3 Novelty. A new Section 2, "Related Work and Delimitation", with nine new
  third-party references and a comparison table, positions the work against the
  three lines you name. The claim is bounded to the scope of that comparison
  and "to the best of our knowledge" has been removed.

- 3.4 Presentation. A JSON-LD listing shows one real shadow component; a worked
  example carries the security posture dimension from HTTP evidence to its
  position on the remediation agenda; the four-versus-ten contrast is now a
  titled paragraph of its own.

- 3.5 Reflexive finding. Developed along both axes: a methodological
  measurement floor (coverage is a joint property of the ecosystem and of its
  publication practices, so a low Psi cannot distinguish weak governance from
  undisclosed governance) and a concrete publication agenda of three low-cost
  acts.

- 4 Minor issues. Over-claims removed, terminology fixed on first mention, all
  figures and tables described in the running text, and the relationship with
  our previous MALTG work stated as an explicit inventory of what is reused and
  what is new.

Two points we flag proactively. Our response to 3.1 deliberately weakens rather
than strengthens several claims about generalisation; we judged a narrow claim
supported by the evidence preferable to a broad one, but will revisit this if
you read it differently. The Discussion was consolidated from eight paragraphs
into five because three restated the reflexive finding and transferability; no
content was removed.

All artefacts needed to reproduce every figure are openly deposited at Zenodo
(DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21942315).

A detailed point-by-point response is attached.

Yours sincerely,
Patricio M. Paccha-Angamarca, on behalf of all authors
